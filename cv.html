Andreas Avgoustis

PhD Candidate — Visual Speech Recognition & Multimodal Learning

RESEARCH PROFILE

PhD candidate working on visual speech recognition for people who have lost their voice after tracheostomy or laryngectomy. My research asks whether a vision–language model, rather than a conventional video classifier, can recognise Greek speech from lip movement alone with enough accuracy to be usable at the bedside. I built the first Greek viseme-level dataset for this population, designed a deterministic preprocessing pipeline that encodes a video segment as a single image a VLM can ingest, and combined parameter-efficient fine-tuning with dense retrieval and a Greek language model as a correction layer. My interests are multimodal representation learning, efficient adaptation of large pretrained models, and evaluation of these systems under the atypical articulation that real patients present.

BiHELab, Ionian University Supervisor: Prof. Themis Exarchos Greek (Native), English (C2)
Research
May 2024 - Present
Doctoral Researcher — Visual Speech Recognition
BiHELab (Bioinformatics & Human Electrophysiology Laboratory), Ionian University
  • Developing a personalized Greek lip-reading system for patients who lose the ability to speak after tracheostomy, from data collection through to evaluation.
  • Built the Greek word-level video corpora the work relies on multiple speakers, several hundred distinct words each together with an automated preprocessing pipeline for mouth-region extraction, temporal resampling and recovery of frames where face detection fails.
  • Designed a viseme-level recognition model a self-supervised vision encoder,a bidirectional recurrent sequence model and a CTC objective that composes words from visual units instead of matching whole recordings, so vocabulary never recorded in training remains reachable: 11.4% top-1 and 20.3% top-5 on unseen words against a chance level of 0.003%.
  • Implemented and benchmarked an alternative retrieval architecture (vision–language model with 4-bit QLoRA, FAISS dense retrieval and a Greek language model as a correction layer) across four speakers, and used the comparison to establish which approach the thesis should pursue.
  • Designed the evaluation protocol: open-vocabulary tests against permutation baselines, multi-seed runs with reported variance, and per-speaker analysis of failure cases.
